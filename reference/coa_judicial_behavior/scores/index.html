    2. coa_judicial_behavior

    Scores the ideology, activism, partisanship, and independence of judges.

    The four scores follow the methods of Corey Rayburn Yung's studies of the judges of the federal courts of appeals:

    • Ideology: Judged by the Company You Keep: An Empirical Study of the Ideologies of Judges on the United States Courts of Appeals, 51 B.C. L. Rev. 1133 (2010).
    • Activism: Flexing Judicial Muscle: An Empirical Study of Judicial Activism in the Federal Courts, 105 Nw. U. L. Rev. 1 (2011).
    • Partisanship and independence: Beyond Ideology: An Empirical Study of Partisanship and Independence in the Federal Courts, 80 Geo. Wash. L. Rev. 505 (2012).
    • All four, revised together: A Typology of Judging Styles, 107 Nw. U. L. Rev. 1757 (2013).

    Each score is computed from a table of votes, with one row for each judge on each case (see coa_judicial_behavior.votes), in three steps: a raw score from rates of agreement and reversal, adjustments for what the judge could not choose (the mix of criminal and civil cases, the parties of the other judges of the "super-panel", and the circuit), and a common scale.

    Ideology is an "agnostic" measure: judges who think alike vote together. Every judge is first given the party of the president who appointed the judge (-1 for a Democrat and 1 for a Republican), and the judge being scored is a blank slate. The Panel Score is the judge's rate of agreement with copanelists appointed by Republicans less the rate with those appointed by Democrats, and the District Score is the same for the district judges whose judgments the judge affirmed (an affirmance is an agreement and a reversal a disagreement). The District Score weights each standard of review by how rarely judgments are reversed under it, since affirming under a deferential standard says less about a judge than reversing under one:

    Panel Score = sum over case types t of
    share(t) * (RateAgree(Rep, t) - RateAgree(Dem, t))
District Score = sum over t of share(t) * sum over standards s of
    w(t, s) * (RateAffirm(Rep, t, s) - RateAffirm(Dem, t, s))

    where share(t) is the average judge's share of criminal or civil cases and w(t, s) is proportional to the judge's votes under each standard times the inverse of the rate of reversal under it (normalized to sum to one within each case type, which keeps the District Score on the scale of the Panel Score, as the 2010 article sought by halving its sum).

    Activism is the difference between how often a judge reversed under deferential standards of review and under de novo review. The de novo rate is the baseline that removes reversals for ideological or other reasons, so what is left is how often the judge failed to defer when the formal model of law says that the judge should have:

    Activism Differential = sum over t of share(t) *
    (ReversalRate(deferential, t) - ReversalRate(de novo, t))

    Partisanship is whether a judge applied the same neutral rule (the standard of review) differently to district judges appointed by the two parties. It is signed by the judge's own party, so that a Democratic appointee who favored Republican district judges is not partisan:

    Partisanship = sum over t of share(t) *
    ((DeferentialReversal(other party, t) - DeferentialReversal(own party, t))
     - (NondeferentialReversal(other party, t) - NondeferentialReversal(own party, t)))

    Independence is how often a judge provoked disagreements (dissents and separate concurrences, by the judge or by a copanelist against the judge's opinion) beyond what ideological differences predict. The expected rates are the fitted values of a linear regression, across the judges scored, of the rates on how far each judge's ideology is from the mean of the judge's circuit:

    Independence = DissentRate + ConcurrenceRate
    - ExpectedDissentRate - ExpectedConcurrenceRate

    A concurrence in part and dissent in part counts as a dissent only.

    The adjustments:

    • Case mix. Every rate is computed separately for criminal and civil cases and weighted by the average judge's shares (share(t)). When a judge has too few votes in a cell (fewer than cell_minimum), the judge's rate in both kinds of cases is used, and else the rate of all judges, which is how the studies "interpolated" thin cells.
    • Super-panel effects. The parties of a judge's copanelists and of the district judge change how the judge votes. Each vote's super-panel is one of six alignments ("RR-R", "RR-D", "DR-R", "DR-D", "DD-R", and "DD-D"). The score that a blank-slate judge would have from the alignments that the judge happened to sit in (computed with the rates of all judges in each alignment, so that offsetting alignments cancel) is subtracted.
    • Circuit. Judges who sat in more than one circuit are assumed to keep their traits, so how their scores differ from circuit to circuit measures the circuits. For each circuit, the traveling judges' votes there are scored together, as one hypothetical judge, and compared with their overall scores weighted by their votes there. The difference is the circuit's differential, which is subtracted from the judges of the circuit: divided by twice the number of judges of the circuit ("per judge", as the 2010 and 2011 articles did) or by the number of traveling votes in the circuit and multiplied by the judge's votes there ("per vote", as the 2012 and 2013 articles did). "full" subtracts the whole differential, weighted by the judge's votes in each circuit, which is not in the articles.
    • Scale. The judges with at least minimum interactions with other judges (two copanelists and a district judge on each vote; the studies required 200) define the scale. Activism, partisanship, and independence run from 0 to 100, and ideology from -100 (liberal) to 100 (conservative). Independence is placed on a logarithmic scale first.

    The 2013 article revised the earlier methods. method = "typology" (the default) follows it, and method = "original" follows each score's first article:

    Choice "original" "typology"
    Panel and District Scores Added (2010). Weighted by the rate of disagreement at each level.
    Zero of the Ideology Score The judge whose rank is the number of Democratic appointees (2010). Equal agreement with both parties (no shift).
    Super-panel effects Ideology and partisanship. Ideology, activism, and partisanship.
    Circuit adjustment Per judge for ideology and activism (2010, 2011), per vote for partisanship (2012). Per vote for all three.
    Independence's expected rates From the scaled Ideology Score (2012). From the adjusted raw Ideology Score.

    Independence has no circuit adjustment in either, because judges seldom write separately when they travel.

    def score_judges(
    votes: pd.DataFrame,
    *,
    method: str = 'typology',
    minimum: int = 200,
    cell_minimum: int = 5,
    provoked: str = 'author',
    circuit: str | None = None,
    log_offset: float = 0.01,
    by: str | None = None) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Returns the four scores of every judge in a table of votes.

    Args:
        votes: a table of votes, with one row for each judge on each case,
            as `review_votes` makes it. It needs "case_id", "judge_nid",
            "judge_party", "court_num", "type_criminal", "review_standard",
            "vote_reversal", "judge_dissented", "judge_concurred",
            "judge_author", "district_party", and "alignment". "judge" and
            "judge_designated" are used if they are there.
        method: "typology" (the 2013 article's methods) or "original" (each
            score's first article). Defaults to "typology".
        minimum: fewest interactions with other judges for a judge to define
            the scales. Defaults to 200, as in the studies.
        cell_minimum: fewest votes in a cell (such as criminal cases under a
            deferential standard) for the judge's own rate in it to be used.
            Defaults to 5.
        provoked: whose separate opinions count toward a judge's
            independence besides the judge's own: "author" (those against
            the opinion that the judge wrote, as the 2012 article describes)
            or "panel" (those against any majority that the judge joined).
            Defaults to "author".
        circuit: the circuit adjustment of ideology, activism, and
            partisanship: "per_judge", "per_vote", "full", or "none".
            Defaults to `None`, in which case each score's adjustment under
            `method` is used.
        log_offset: what is added to the shifted raw Independence Scores
            before their logarithm is taken, so that the lowest is not zero.
            Defaults to 0.01 (one percentage point).
        by: name of a column (such as "year") whose values are scored
            separately, as the studies scored one year. Defaults to `None`,
            in which case every vote is scored together.

    Raises:
        ValueError: if `method`, `provoked`, or `circuit` is not one of its
            choices.
        KeyError: if `votes` lacks a column that is needed.

    Returns:
        A table with a row for each judge (and value of `by`), sorted from
            the most liberal Ideology Score. Its columns are described in the
            documentation of the project.

    """
    _check(method, METHODS, 'method')
    _check(provoked, _PROVOKED, 'provoked')
    if circuit is not None:
        _check(circuit, _CIRCUIT_METHODS, 'circuit')

    def score(frame: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
        return _score(
            frame, method = method, minimum = int(minimum),
            cell_minimum = int(cell_minimum), provoked = provoked,
            circuit = circuit, log_offset = float(log_offset))

    if by is None:
        return score(votes)
    tables = []
    for value, group in votes.groupby(by, sort = True):
        table = score(group)
        table.insert(1, by, value)
        tables.append(table)
    return pd.concat(tables, ignore_index = True)
