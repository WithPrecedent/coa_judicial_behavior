    Advanced User Guide¶

    This guide gives the formulas of the four scores as score_judges computes them, the differences between the methods of the articles, and the places where the articles left a choice open and how this project made it.

    Notation¶

    For a judge, t is a case type (criminal or civil) and s a standard of review (deferential, nondeferential, or unclear). share(t) is the average judge's share of votes in criminal or civil cases (the mean over judges of each judge's share), which the articles called "the distribution of the average judge." Every rate is a judge's mean in a cell, such as the judge's rate of reversal in civil cases under deferential standards.

    Thin cells. When a judge has fewer than cell_minimum votes (5 by default) in a cell, the judge's rate in that cell for criminal and civil cases together is used, and if that is thin too, the rate of all judges in the cell. The articles "interpolated based upon expected and actual scores with that judge's case mix"; this is one way to do that, and it never lets a single vote set a rate.

    Ideology¶

    Panel Score = sum_t share(t) * (Agree(Rep, t) - Agree(Dem, t))

    Agree(P, t) is the share of the judge's pairs with copanelists of party P in which the two agreed (both joined the majority, or both dissented).

    District Score = sum_t share(t) * sum_s w(t, s) * (Affirm(Rep, t, s) - Affirm(Dem, t, s))

    Affirm(P, t, s) is the judge's rate of affirming district judges of party P. The weight of a standard is

    w(t, s) = W(t, s) * n(t, s) / sum_s' W(t, s') * n(t, s')
W(t, s) = (1 / ReversalRate(t, s)) / sum_s' (1 / ReversalRate(t, s'))

    where n(t, s) is the judge's number of votes reviewing district judges in the cell and ReversalRate(t, s) the rate of all judges. In the 2010 article's example, the weights of criminal cases were 0.479 (deferential), 0.241 (nondeferential), and 0.280 (unclear), which is what inverse rates of reversal give. The article's expression divided the weighted sum by the total number of votes and then halved it "to keep the District Score on the same relative scale"; normalizing the weights within each case type does that exactly, for any mix of votes.

    The raw score combines the two:

    Method Raw Ideology Score
    original (2010) Panel Score + District Score
    typology (2013) (d_panel * Panel Score + d_district * District Score) / (d_panel + d_district), where d is the rate of disagreement at each level among all judges ("combined based upon the rate of disagreement at each level").

    Zero and scale. The 2010 article put zero at the mean of the k-th and (k+1)-th most liberal judges, where k is the number of Democratic appointees, and scaled so that the judge farthest from zero is at -100 or 100. The 2013 article kept zero where it is (equal agreement with both parties) and chose "the multiplier ... that would either move the highest score to 100 or lowest score to [-]100."

    Activism¶

    Activism Differential = sum_t share(t) * (Reversal(deferential, t) - Reversal(nondeferential, t))

    Judge Wood's example in the 2011 article (4 reversals in 53 deferential votes and 13 in 49 de novo votes) is a test of this project. The article's case-mix expression estimated a judge's criminal and civil rates from the judge's totals and the average judge's distributions, because its data were thin; this project computes the rates in each case type directly, with the fallbacks above. Votes under an unclear standard do not count.

    The 2011 article made no super-panel adjustment; the 2013 article did, and so does method = "typology".

    Partisanship¶

    Partisanship = sum_t share(t) * ((DefRev(other, t) - DefRev(own, t)) - (NonRev(other, t) - NonRev(own, t)))

    DefRev(other, t) is the judge's rate of reversing district judges appointed by the other party under deferential standards, and so on. This is the 2012 article's raw formula (with Democratic and Republican district judges) multiplied by the judge's own party (1 for a Republican appointee and -1 for a Democratic one), which is the article's "one unique alteration": a Democratic appointee who favors Republican district judges is not partisan. Writing it with "own" and "other" party lets the traveling judges of a circuit, who belong to both parties, be scored together.

    Independence¶

    Independence = sum_t share(t) * (Dissents(t) + Concurrences(t) - E[Dissents(t)] - E[Concurrences(t)])

    Dissents(t) is the judge's dissents per vote plus, by default, the dissents of copanelists against opinions that the judge wrote (provoked = "author", the 2012 article's "writes an opinion that draws a dissenting opinion"). provoked = "panel" counts dissents against any majority that the judge joined, which is how the 2013 article's footnote ("the difference between dissenting and being dissented against is simply how the third judge on the panel voted") can be read. A concurrence in part and dissent in part is a dissent only.

    The expected rates are the fitted values of a linear regression, across the judges who define the scales, of each rate on the absolute distance between the judge's ideology and the mean ideology of the judges of the judge's circuit. The 2012 article used the scaled Ideology Score; the 2013 article used "the degree to which a judge's Raw Ideology Score differed from the mean of the circuit," which method = "typology" uses (the adjusted raw score, before scaling).

    The raw scores are shifted so that the lowest is log_offset (0.01 by default), and their logarithm is scaled from 0 to 100. The articles placed the scores "on a logarithmic scale (to create a normal distribution)" without saying how they made them positive; the offset is that choice, and a smaller one spreads out the lowest scores.

    Adjustments¶

    Super-panel effects. Each vote has an alignment: the parties of the two copanelists and of the district judge (RR-R, RR-D, DR-R, DR-D, DD-R, DD-D). For each alignment, case type, and standard, the rates of agreement and reversal of all judges are what a blank-slate judge would do there. The score is computed again with those rates in place of the judge's own, and that "expected" score is subtracted. So alignments that offset each other (thirty RR-R panels against ten DD-D panels, in the articles' example) cancel, and only the excess pulls the score, as the articles described.

    Circuit. For each circuit, the votes there of judges who sat in more than one circuit are scored together as one hypothetical judge ("actual"), and compared with those judges' overall scores weighted by their votes there ("expected"). The differential, actual less expected, is subtracted:

    circuit Amount subtracted from a judge's score Used by
    per_judge The differential of the judge's home circuit / (2 * the judges of the circuit) 2010 (ideology) and 2011 (activism)
    per_vote The sum over circuits of the differential / the traveling votes there * the judge's votes there 2012 (partisanship) and 2013 (all three)
    full The sum over circuits of the differential * the judge's share of votes there Not in the articles
    none Nothing Independence, in both

    The per_vote formula follows the 2012 and 2013 appendices literally ("the raw adjustment number was then divided by the number of traveling votes in the circuit ... Then each judge within the circuit would have the circuit adjustment applied based upon the number of votes he or she issued"). When a circuit has few traveling votes and its judges many votes, it can be larger than the differential itself; the {score}_circuit column shows its size, and circuit = "full" is the alternative that never is.

    Scale. The judges with at least minimum interactions (200 in the articles) define every scale. Activism, partisanship, and independence are shifted so that the lowest of them is 0 and multiplied so that the highest is 100; other judges can fall outside 0 to 100.

    Methods at a glance¶

    Choice method = "original" method = "typology"
    Raw Ideology Score Panel + District Weighted by the rates of disagreement
    Ideology's zero The k-th judge (k = Democratic appointees) Equal agreement with both parties
    Super-panel adjustment Ideology, partisanship Ideology, activism, partisanship
    Circuit adjustment Per judge (ideology, activism), per vote (partisanship) Per vote
    Independence's expected rates Scaled Ideology Score Adjusted raw Ideology Score

    Scoring periods¶

    The articles scored one year. by = "year" (or any column) scores each value separately, with its own context (shares, weights, alignments, travelers, and scale), so the scores of different years are each relative to their own judges. A judge needs minimum interactions within a period to define its scales, so with one year at a time a lower minimum may be needed in small circuits.

    Judging styles¶

    classify uses scipy's Ward linkage on the four scores (the absolute Ideology Score, which is from 0 to 100, and the other three), cuts the tree into clusters groups, and numbers them in the order of the tree's leaves. With nine clusters, each is matched to one of the article's styles by the smallest total squared distance between the clusters' standardized means and the styles' profiles (read from the article's descriptions in Part III.B: "high" is about one standard deviation, "highest" or "lowest" about one and a half). The "distance" column of the "typology" table says how well each cluster fits its style.
