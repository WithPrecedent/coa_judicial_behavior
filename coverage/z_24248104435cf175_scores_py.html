1"""Scores the ideology, activism, partisanship, and independence of judges. 

2 

3The four scores follow the methods of Corey Rayburn Yung's studies of the 

4judges of the federal courts of appeals: 

5 

6* **Ideology**: *Judged by the Company You Keep: An Empirical Study of the 

7 Ideologies of Judges on the United States Courts of Appeals*, 51 B.C. L. 

8 Rev. 1133 (2010). 

9* **Activism**: *Flexing Judicial Muscle: An Empirical Study of Judicial 

10 Activism in the Federal Courts*, 105 Nw. U. L. Rev. 1 (2011). 

11* **Partisanship and independence**: *Beyond Ideology: An Empirical Study of 

12 Partisanship and Independence in the Federal Courts*, 80 Geo. Wash. L. 

13 Rev. 505 (2012). 

14* **All four, revised together**: *A Typology of Judging Styles*, 107 Nw. U. 

15 L. Rev. 1757 (2013). 

16 

17Each score is computed from a table of votes, with one row for each judge on 

18each case (see `coa_judicial_behavior.votes`), in three steps: a raw score 

19from rates of agreement and reversal, adjustments for what the judge could 

20not choose (the mix of criminal and civil cases, the parties of the other 

21judges of the "super-panel", and the circuit), and a common scale. 

22 

23**Ideology** is an "agnostic" measure: judges who think alike vote together. 

24Every judge is first given the party of the president who appointed the 

25judge (-1 for a Democrat and 1 for a Republican), and the judge being scored 

26is a blank slate. The Panel Score is the judge's rate of agreement with 

27copanelists appointed by Republicans less the rate with those appointed by 

28Democrats, and the District Score is the same for the district judges whose 

29judgments the judge affirmed (an affirmance is an agreement and a reversal a 

30disagreement). The District Score weights each standard of review by how 

31rarely judgments are reversed under it, since affirming under a deferential 

32standard says less about a judge than reversing under one: 

33 

34 Panel Score = sum over case types t of 

35 share(t) * (RateAgree(Rep, t) - RateAgree(Dem, t)) 

36 District Score = sum over t of share(t) * sum over standards s of 

37 w(t, s) * (RateAffirm(Rep, t, s) - RateAffirm(Dem, t, s)) 

38 

39where `share(t)` is the average judge's share of criminal or civil cases and 

40`w(t, s)` is proportional to the judge's votes under each standard times the 

41inverse of the rate of reversal under it (normalized to sum to one within 

42each case type, which keeps the District Score on the scale of the Panel 

43Score, as the 2010 article sought by halving its sum). 

44 

45**Activism** is the difference between how often a judge reversed under 

46deferential standards of review and under de novo review. The de novo rate 

47is the baseline that removes reversals for ideological or other reasons, so 

48what is left is how often the judge failed to defer when the formal model of 

49law says that the judge should have: 

50 

51 Activism Differential = sum over t of share(t) * 

52 (ReversalRate(deferential, t) - ReversalRate(de novo, t)) 

53 

54**Partisanship** is whether a judge applied the same neutral rule (the 

55standard of review) differently to district judges appointed by the two 

56parties. It is signed by the judge's own party, so that a Democratic 

57appointee who favored Republican district judges is not partisan: 

58 

59 Partisanship = sum over t of share(t) * 

60 ((DeferentialReversal(other party, t) - DeferentialReversal(own party, t)) 

61 - (NondeferentialReversal(other party, t) - NondeferentialReversal(own party, t))) 

62 

63**Independence** is how often a judge provoked disagreements (dissents and 

64separate concurrences, by the judge or by a copanelist against the judge's 

65opinion) beyond what ideological differences predict. The expected rates are 

66the fitted values of a linear regression, across the judges scored, of the 

67rates on how far each judge's ideology is from the mean of the judge's 

68circuit: 

69 

70 Independence = DissentRate + ConcurrenceRate 

71 - ExpectedDissentRate - ExpectedConcurrenceRate 

72 

73A concurrence in part and dissent in part counts as a dissent only. 

74 

75The adjustments: 

76 

77* **Case mix.** Every rate is computed separately for criminal and civil 

78 cases and weighted by the average judge's shares (`share(t)`). When a 

79 judge has too few votes in a cell (fewer than `cell_minimum`), the 

80 judge's rate in both kinds of cases is used, and else the rate of all 

81 judges, which is how the studies "interpolated" thin cells. 

82* **Super-panel effects.** The parties of a judge's copanelists and of the 

83 district judge change how the judge votes. Each vote's super-panel is one 

84 of six alignments ("RR-R", "RR-D", "DR-R", "DR-D", "DD-R", and "DD-D"). 

85 The score that a blank-slate judge would have from the alignments that 

86 the judge happened to sit in (computed with the rates of all judges in 

87 each alignment, so that offsetting alignments cancel) is subtracted. 

88* **Circuit.** Judges who sat in more than one circuit are assumed to keep 

89 their traits, so how their scores differ from circuit to circuit measures 

90 the circuits. For each circuit, the traveling judges' votes there are 

91 scored together, as one hypothetical judge, and compared with their 

92 overall scores weighted by their votes there. The difference is the 

93 circuit's differential, which is subtracted from the judges of the 

94 circuit: divided by twice the number of judges of the circuit ("per 

95 judge", as the 2010 and 2011 articles did) or by the number of traveling 

96 votes in the circuit and multiplied by the judge's votes there ("per 

97 vote", as the 2012 and 2013 articles did). "full" subtracts the whole 

98 differential, weighted by the judge's votes in each circuit, which is not 

99 in the articles. 

100* **Scale.** The judges with at least `minimum` interactions with other 

101 judges (two copanelists and a district judge on each vote; the studies 

102 required 200) define the scale. Activism, partisanship, and independence 

103 run from 0 to 100, and ideology from -100 (liberal) to 100 

104 (conservative). Independence is placed on a logarithmic scale first. 

105 

106The 2013 article revised the earlier methods. `method = "typology"` (the 

107default) follows it, and `method = "original"` follows each score's first 

108article: 

109 

110| Choice | "original" | "typology" | 

111| --- | --- | --- | 

112| Panel and District Scores | Added (2010). | Weighted by the rate of disagreement at each level. | 

113| Zero of the Ideology Score | The judge whose rank is the number of Democratic appointees (2010). | Equal agreement with both parties (no shift). | 

114| Super-panel effects | Ideology and partisanship. | Ideology, activism, and partisanship. | 

115| Circuit adjustment | Per judge for ideology and activism (2010, 2011), per vote for partisanship (2012). | Per vote for all three. | 

116| Independence's expected rates | From the scaled Ideology Score (2012). | From the adjusted raw Ideology Score. | 

117 

118Independence has no circuit adjustment in either, because judges seldom 

119write separately when they travel. 

120 

121Contents: 

122 METHODS: the versions of the methods. 

123 SCORES: the names of the four scores. 

124 score_judges: returns the four scores of every judge in a table of 

125 votes. 

126

235 votes: pd.DataFrame, 

236 *, 

237 method: str = 'typology', 

238 minimum: int = 200, 

239 cell_minimum: int = 5,

244 """Returns the four scores of every judge in a table of votes. 

245 

246 Args: 

247 votes: a table of votes, with one row for each judge on each case, 

248 as `review_votes` makes it. It needs "case_id", "judge_nid", 

249 "judge_party", "court_num", "type_criminal", "review_standard", 

250 "vote_reversal", "judge_dissented", "judge_concurred", 

251 "judge_author", "district_party", and "alignment". "judge" and 

252 "judge_designated" are used if they are there. 

253 method: "typology" (the 2013 article's methods) or "original" (each 

254 score's first article). Defaults to "typology". 

255 minimum: fewest interactions with other judges for a judge to define 

256 the scales. Defaults to 200, as in the studies. 

257 cell_minimum: fewest votes in a cell (such as criminal cases under a 

258 deferential standard) for the judge's own rate in it to be used. 

259 Defaults to 5. 

260 provoked: whose separate opinions count toward a judge's 

261 independence besides the judge's own: "author" (those against 

262 the opinion that the judge wrote, as the 2012 article describes) 

263 or "panel" (those against any majority that the judge joined). 

264 Defaults to "author". 

265 circuit: the circuit adjustment of ideology, activism, and 

266 partisanship: "per_judge", "per_vote", "full", or "none". 

267 Defaults to `None`, in which case each score's adjustment under 

268 `method` is used. 

269 log_offset: what is added to the shifted raw Independence Scores 

270 before their logarithm is taken, so that the lowest is not zero. 

271 Defaults to 0.01 (one percentage point). 

272 by: name of a column (such as "year") whose values are scored 

273 separately, as the studies scored one year. Defaults to `None`, 

274 in which case every vote is scored together. 

275 

276 Raises: 

277 ValueError: if `method`, `provoked`, or `circuit` is not one of its 

278 choices. 

279 KeyError: if `votes` lacks a column that is needed. 

280 

281 Returns: 

282 A table with a row for each judge (and value of `by`), sorted from 

283 the most liberal Ideology Score. Its columns are described in the 

284 documentation of the project. 

285 

286 """ 

287 _check(method, METHODS, 'method') 

288 _check(provoked, _PROVOKED, 'provoked') 

289 if circuit is not None: 

290 _check(circuit, _CIRCUIT_METHODS, 'circuit') 

291 

292 def score(frame: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame: 

293 return _score( 

294 frame, method = method, minimum = int(minimum), 

295 cell_minimum = int(cell_minimum), provoked = provoked, 

296 circuit = circuit, log_offset = float(log_offset)) 

297 

298 if by is None: 

299 return score(votes) 

300 tables = [] 

301 for value, group in votes.groupby(by, sort = True): 

302 table = score(group) 

303 table.insert(1, by, value) 

304 tables.append(table) 

305 return pd.concat(tables, ignore_index = True)

403 context: _Context, 

404 *,

406 """Returns each judge's raw Activism Differential. 

407 

408 Args: 

409 votes: the votes to score, with "unit".

414 

415 Returns:

417 

418 """ 

419 value = 'expected_reversed' if expected else 'reversed' 

420 reviewed = votes[

437def _ideology( 

438 votes: pd.DataFrame, 

439 pairs: pd.DataFrame, 

440 context: _Context, 

441 method: str, 

442 *, 

443 expected: bool = False) -> pd.DataFrame: 

444 """Returns each judge's Panel, District, and raw Ideology Scores. 

445 

446 Args: 

447 votes: the votes to score, with "unit". 

448 pairs: the pairs of votes and copanelists. 

449 context: what the judges are compared with. 

450 method: "typology" or "original". 

451 expected: whether to score the agreements that a blank-slate judge 

452 would have in each vote's super-panel instead of the judge's. 

453 Defaults to `False`. 

454 

455 Returns: 

456 A table with a row for each judge (or "unit") and the columns 

457 "panel", "district", and "raw". 

458 

459 """ 

460 units = pd.Index(votes['unit'].unique()) 

461 together = _paired(pairs, votes) 

462 value = 'expected_agree' if expected else 'agree' 

463 rates, _ = _cell_rates( 

464 together, context.pairs, value, ['criminal', 'colleague_party'], 

465 [list(_CASE_TYPES), list(_PARTIES)], minimum = context.minimum) 

466 panel = pd.Series(0.0, index = units) 

467 for kind in _CASE_TYPES: 

468 difference = ( 

469 _cell(rates, criminal = kind, colleague_party = 1.0) 

470 - _cell(rates, criminal = kind, colleague_party = -1.0)) 

471 panel = panel.add(context.shares[kind] * difference, fill_value = 0) 

472 reviewed = votes[ 

473 votes['district_party'].notna() & votes['affirmed'].notna()] 

474 reference = context.votes[ 

475 context.votes['district_party'].notna() 

476 & context.votes['affirmed'].notna()] 

477 value = 'expected_affirmed' if expected else 'affirmed' 

478 district = pd.Series(0.0, index = units) 

479 if len(reviewed): 

480 rates, counts = _cell_rates( 

481 reviewed, reference, value, 

482 ['criminal', 'standard', 'district_party'], 

483 [list(_CASE_TYPES), list(_STANDARDS), list(_PARTIES)], 

484 minimum = context.minimum) 

485 numbers = counts.groupby( 

486 level = ['unit', 'criminal', 'standard']).sum() 

487 overall = reference.groupby(['criminal', 'standard']).size() 

488 for kind in _CASE_TYPES: 

489 total = pd.Series(0.0, index = rates.index.unique('unit')) 

490 weight = pd.Series(0.0, index = total.index) 

491 fallback_total = pd.Series(0.0, index = total.index) 

492 fallback_weight = 0.0 

493 for standard in _STANDARDS: 

494 difference = ( 

495 _cell(rates, criminal = kind, standard = standard, 

496 district_party = 1.0) 

497 - _cell(rates, criminal = kind, standard = standard, 

498 district_party = -1.0)) 

499 w = float(context.weights[(kind, standard)]) 

500 n = _cell(numbers, criminal = kind, standard = standard) 

501 total = total.add(w * n * difference, fill_value = 0) 

502 weight = weight.add(w * n, fill_value = 0) 

503 n_all = float(overall.get((kind, standard), 0)) 

504 fallback_total = fallback_total.add( 

505 w * n_all * difference, fill_value = 0) 

506 fallback_weight += w * n_all 

507 within = (total / weight).where(weight > 0) 

508 if fallback_weight > 0: 

509 within = within.fillna(fallback_total / fallback_weight) 

510 district = district.add( 

511 context.shares[kind] * within.fillna(0), fill_value = 0) 

512 if method == 'typology': 

513 levels = context.panel_disagreement + context.district_disagreement 

514 raw = ( 

515 (context.panel_disagreement * panel 

516 + context.district_disagreement * district) / levels 

517 if levels > 0 else panel + district) 

518 else: 

519 raw = panel + district 

520 return pd.DataFrame({'panel': panel, 'district': district, 'raw': raw})

523def _independence( 

524 table: pd.DataFrame, 

525 votes: pd.DataFrame, 

526 context: _Context, 

527 ideology: pd.Series, 

528 log_offset: float) -> None: 

529 """Adds each judge's Independence Score to the table of scores. 

530 

531 Args: 

532 table: the table of scores, with "judge_nid", "circuit", and 

533 "qualified". It is changed in place. 

534 votes: every vote, with "unit". 

535 context: what the judges are compared with. 

536 ideology: the measure of each judge's ideology (in the order of 

537 `table`) whose distance from the circuit's mean predicts the 

538 judge's separate opinions. 

539 log_offset: what is added before the logarithm is taken. 

540 

541 """ 

542 units = table['judge_nid'] 

543 qualified = table['qualified'].to_numpy() 

544 measure = pd.Series(ideology.to_numpy(), index = units) 

545 circuits = pd.Series(table['circuit'].to_numpy(), index = units) 

546 means = measure[qualified].groupby(circuits[qualified]).mean() 

547 deviation = (measure - circuits.map(means)).abs() 

548 deviation = deviation.fillna(deviation[qualified].mean()) 

549 raw = pd.Series(0.0, index = units) 

550 for column, kind in ( 

551 ('dissent_events', 'dissent'), ('concurrence_events', 'concurrence')): 

552 rates, _ = _cell_rates( 

553 votes, context.votes, column, ['criminal'], 

554 [list(_CASE_TYPES)], minimum = context.minimum) 

555 observed = pd.Series(0.0, index = units) 

556 predicted = pd.Series(0.0, index = units) 

557 for case_type in _CASE_TYPES: 

558 rate = _cell(rates, criminal = case_type).reindex(units) 

559 fitted = _fit(deviation, rate, qualified) 

560 observed += context.shares[case_type] * rate.fillna(0) 

561 predicted += context.shares[case_type] * fitted

701 counted = table[table['qualified']] if table['qualified'].any() else ( 

702 table) 

703 judges = counted.groupby('circuit').size()

705 differentials.get(c, 0.0) / (2 * judges.get(c, 1))
